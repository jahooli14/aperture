For Katie · a worked example

What your notes already knew

Six months of meeting notes — invented, but the kind you'd actually write after an hour with a CUO. Two hundred of them, and the diary alongside. None of this is about remembering. You'd remember every one of these conversations. It's about what can't be seen from inside a conversation: patterns that only exist between people who have never met, in words that never repeat.

Fictitious clients and colleagues throughout — not your accounts. Every quotation below is checked word-for-word against the note it cites; a bracketed letter marks the one permitted edit, capitalising a phrase lifted from mid-sentence.

An echo

Three clients told you where the market was heading. All three got it from you.

  1. 14 APR

    Your firm publishes its specialty outlook

    Headline claim: half of Lloyd's managing agents gone by 2030.

    “I have no idea whether that's true. Nobody I've asked internally knows where the number came from.”

    — your own note, the day it went out.

  2. 12 MAY

    Client breakfast

    Forty in the room. The report goes to a list of three hundred.

  3. 10 JUN

    Piotr Zawada · Bellwether MGAwas in the room

    “[Y]our report says half the managing agents disappear by 2030.”
  4. 24 JUN

    Ivan Kroll · Pemberton Brokingwas in the room

    Repeats the number back. Attributes it to “everyone”.

  5. 15 JUL

    Tobias Greer · Orwell Marineon the list

    “[Y]ou lot are saying half the managing agents go by 2030.”

Why it stays hidden

Nothing in any of those three conversations marks the number as yours. By the third telling it has stopped being a report and become what everyone thinks — Ivan didn't say he'd read it, he said everyone was saying it. The only trace is the guest list. Left alone, your own unsourced number comes back to the firm as evidence that the market agrees.

A contradiction

Two CUOs took opposite sides six weeks apart. The answer is also in your notes.

  1. 19 MAR

    Marcus Deyn · Ravensbourne, CUO

    “The data's finally good enough that we can write parametric on things that aren't windstorm. I've got a team on it.”
  2. 5 MAY

    Ana Castellane · Harrow Point, Head of Specialty

    “Parametric is a solution looking for a problem … The problem is the buyer thinks they bought insurance and they bought a bet.”
  3. 13 MAY

    Marcus Deyn · pushed on basis risk

    “[T]he reason it's failed before is people sold it as insurance when it's a hedge. We're selling it as a hedge.”
  4. 15 MAY

    Graham Tull · Duraflex, a buyer

    “I've got a real problem, I don't need a clever one.”
  5. 25 AUG

    Beatrice Ofori · Norton Hale, a buyer

    Puts port delay at £4m last year, none of it insured.

    “If someone can pay me when the port backs up I don't care what they call it.”

Why it stays hidden

Ana is right about Graham. Marcus is right about Beatrice. The line between them is whether the buyer already counts the loss — Beatrice has a figure for it, Graham doesn't. But two of these are underwriting views and two are buying conversations, filed under four accounts across five months. Nothing puts them on one page, and the disagreement isn't the finding — the resolution is, and it only appears with all four side by side.

A first voice

Klaus called it in April. Four people have since told you it was their own idea.

  1. 21 APR

    Klaus Beringer · Trans-Meridian Re

    “This will show up at one-one. Not this one-one, the one after.”

    Asked who else was saying it: “Almost nobody, which is what worries me.”

  2. 20 MAY

    Sofia Marchetti · Kestrel Re

    “[T]he numbers look too good.”
  3. 16 JUN

    Derek Ainsworth · Castleford Mutual

    “[T]he lawyers have got more organised.”
  4. 24 JUN

    Ivan Kroll · Pemberton Broking

    “The markets aren't explaining it, they're just asking for more.”
  5. 13 JUL

    Hal Brennan · your New York officearrived independently

    “[T]he severity numbers out of the US right now are not something the London market has priced.”

    Has verdict data he'll share. Has never met Klaus.

  6. 11 AUG

    Giles Overton · Thackeray 2884

    “[T]he direction is one way and it's the same direction every quarter.”

    Thinks it's his own book.

Why it stays hidden

Not one of the four mentions Klaus. Each describes it as something happening in their own book, in their own vocabulary — Giles thinks it's his reserving. Being first isn't a property of any single conversation. It only exists once six of them are sorted by date, which nothing prompts you to do. The useful part isn't the call; it's knowing whose read to weight next time.

An absence

Nobody has mentioned the June deadline since the first week of July.

  1. 18 MAR

    Rob Gilder · Harrow Point, CFO

    “We are going to make it but I don't have a spare person in the building between now and July.”
  2. 25 MAR

    Nils Ekström · Regulatory, internal

    Hires three people for the deadline work.

  3. 15 APR

    Piotr Zawada · Bellwether MGA

    “[I]t's landed on underwriting, not just finance, which nobody expected.”
  4. 28 APR – 6 MAY

    Ines Delacroix · Pallister · Peter Ngo · Stanhope

    Five organisations in seven weeks. Then it stops.

  5. JUL – SEP

    Seventy notes. Not one mention.

  6. 8 SEP

    Nils Ekström · Regulatory, internal

    The three hires have nothing booked past October. In June he'd already said it:

    “I need a second act and I don't have one.”

Why it stays hidden

There is no note saying nobody mentions this anymore. An absence isn't recorded anywhere — it's the shape of the space between April and August, and it can only be found by comparing two months against each other. Nils asked you in June what you were hearing that was regulatory-shaped. The answer was already in your notes. It just wasn't in any one of them.

And what it declined to say

A broker's system migration

Five mentions since March — the single loudest topic in six months of notes.

Three people, one firm. Not a market. Stayed quiet.

The AI trend that wasn't one

Three clients in four weeks. One hated a vendor demo, one is building submission triage, one is running claims fraud detection.

Same word, three unrelated subjects. Stayed quiet.

The harder half

Anything will find you patterns if you let it. The discipline is in the refusals — and on most weeks, in a corpus this size, the honest output is nothing at all.

Where it surfaces

Findings attach themselves to the diary. Three days before a meeting, one thing worth carrying in — never a summary of the last one.
